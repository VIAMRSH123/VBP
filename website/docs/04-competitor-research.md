# Competitive Audit — Qatar Food Trading

Researched 18 Oct 2026. The ten names came from the brief's research shortlist; they are treated as
a shortlist to verify, **not** as a verified ranking. Confidence is stated per entry, and nothing in
this document is published on the public site.

## 1. Bradma Qatar Food — the real benchmark

- **URL** `https://www.bradmagroup.com.qa/` — verified live.
- **Type** Diversified group (Food · Automotive · Building Materials · more). Food is division 01.
- **What they claim** "Feeding Qatar since 1970"; "an ISO 22000:2005 certified organization and is
  the largest importer of rice & pulses in the State of Qatar"; mentions trading through the
  blockade.
- **Design** Light corporate template. Serif welcome headline over a stock rice-bowl photo,
  centred paragraphs, numbered division list, Sign In button. Clean, dated, no motion language.
- **Strong** Three credibility assets stated *above the fold*: a date (1970), a certification
  (ISO 22000), and a category claim (largest importer of rice & pulses). Clients page. Careers.
- **Weak** Reads as a holding company, not a food specialist — rice competes for attention with
  brake pads and scaffolding. No product-level pages. No packaging photography. Generic imagery.
- **Opportunity for TFS** TFS is the *opposite* shape: a rice specialist that owns its brands.
  Depth beats breadth — give every SKU a page with its real pack, which Bradma cannot match.
- **Confidence** High — read directly off their homepage.

## 2. Royal Doha Trading / "Royal Food Center"

- **URL** `https://www.royaldohatrading.com/` — verified live.
- **Type** Food distribution and wholesale; own "Royal" brand across snacks, commodities, oil,
  pickles, juices. Rice/grains/pulses/spices are one line among many.
- **Design** Dark hero, a wall of packet photography, neon-green "WE ASSURE 100% QUALITY
  PRODUCTS", WhatsApp float, "Store" and "Trusted Brands" sections.
- **Strong** Product-forward hero that shows the actual range; WhatsApp contact matches how Gulf
  trade actually buys.
- **Weak** **Three "testimonials" carry the identical sentence under three different names** —
  visibly fabricated, and it costs them trust. Typography is unconsidered; no hierarchy.
- **Opportunity for TFS** Be the one with no invented social proof. Credibility through specifics
  (pack sizes, origin, sortex grade), not through made-up quotes.
- **Confidence** High — read directly.

## 3. Progressive Marketing W.L.L.

- **URL** `https://progressiveqatar.com/` — **the site is currently broken**: HTTP 500 to a direct
  fetch, blank white page in a real browser.
- **Type** FMCG distributor; trade press describes it as part of the Family Food Centre Group with
  47+ years in retail/wholesale/distribution and a HORECA push (Unikai ice cream launch).
- **Opportunity for TFS** A competitor with scale and no working website. Simply being reachable
  and credible online is a differentiator in this market.
- **Confidence** Medium on the corporate facts (trade press, not their own site, which is down);
  high on the site being broken — observed twice, two different ways.

## 4. LuLu Hypermarket Trading Co. W.L.L.

- **Type** Retail hypermarket chain, not a distributor. **Adjacent, not a competitor** — it is a
  *customer channel*: the shelf TFS's retail packs would sit on.
- **URL** `luluhypermarket.com/en-qa` returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so it was not
  audited. No URL is asserted as verified here.
- **Confidence** Low on specifics; high on the category judgment.

## 5–10. Not verifiable

**International Foodstuff Group (IFG)** · **Greenland Food Stuff W.L.L.** · **Taj Al Baraka
Foodstuff Trading** · **Abdullah Ali Sons for Foodstuffs** · **Al Majid Jawad Amjad W.L.L.**

Multiple searches returned no official website for any of these under those exact names. Results
were directory aggregators, unrelated organisations (e.g. the Islamic Organization for Food
Security), or similarly-named companies in other emirates. **No URLs are invented for them.**

What this absence is worth knowing: most of the Doha foodstuff trade has either no web presence or
a directory listing someone else wrote. The bar in this market is low, and the gap is wide open.

- **Confidence** High that they are not findable from the open web; unknown whether they exist
  under other registered names. A Qatar MOCI commercial-registry lookup would settle it.

## What the audit changes about the design

1. **Nobody in this set treats product packaging as photography.** Everyone uses generic rice-bowl
   stock. TFS has 1000×1000 pack renders — they become the hero, not decoration.
2. **Credibility in this market is stated, not shown.** Bradma states a year and an ISO number.
   TFS can state things it can actually prove from the packs: Korutla origin, Satake sortex, 18/20/
   35 kg trade sizes, trilingual labelling. Specifics instead of adjectives.
3. **Nobody has a product-detail layer.** Every competitor stops at a category. Seven rice pages
   with real pack art is a structural advantage, and it is also the SEO moat.
4. **Fake social proof is the local norm.** Avoiding it is a differentiator in itself.
5. **The enquiry path is weak everywhere** — a generic form or a WhatsApp button. A qualified B2B
   enquiry form (business type, category, volume context) is better than anything in the set.
